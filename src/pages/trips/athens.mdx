## Day by Day

**Day 1 · Arrival · Agora of Athens**

We landed at Athens airport at 12:30 and caught the metro (blue line) into Athens. It involved a change and took about 40 minutes. We arrived in Athens at about 13:30 and had some lunch, then went to check in at our hotel at 14:30 and drop off our bags.

**How to get from Athens airport to the city centre:**

1. Follow the signs from arrivals to the metro station (Line 3, blue), heading for Syntagma.
2. At Syntagma, change to Line 2 (red) for one stop to Acropoli.

A taxi would cost about €40 to €50. The metro cost about €9, and no ticket was needed, as you just tap in and tap out.

We were staying at [Coco-Mat Athens BC](https://www.staycocomat.com/), which is right next to the Acropolis Museum and the entrance to the Acropolis. The hotel does have rooms with views of the Parthenon, but we didn't have one of these. Nevertheless, the room was great, clean and comfy, which was pretty much all we needed. We did have a small balcony to sit out on, with fantastic views of the car rental places across the street. After having a sit down and a shower, we went for a walk around town.

We got to the Agora of Athens at 16:00 and left at 17:00. Entry was €20 per person. We had a drink at one of the many restaurants at the entrance to the Agora, all of which seemed to be serving happy hour at that time until at least 19:00, with Aperol spritz for €7. There were views over the Agora and towards the Parthenon.

We were really tired from our early start, so we made a short walk back to our hotel, checking out some of the shops along the way.

In the evening we went for dinner at To Kati Allo, a small family-run taverna just around the corner from our hotel. It serves traditional, home-style Greek cooking, and there is no fixed menu, so the dishes change from day to day. The waiter was great and talked us through what was on offer that evening before we ordered. The food was simple, inexpensive and very tasty, coming to just over €10 per person with beer on top. They don't have a website, but if you search for the name on Google Maps you'll find plenty of reviews and directions. It's at Chatzichristou 12, near the Acropolis Museum.

**Day 2 · Temple of Olympian Zeus · Panathenaic Stadium · Lycabettus · Acropolis**

Our hotel included breakfast, which we made good use of. It was a buffet with a generous spread, including eggs, meats, pastries and sweets. We left the hotel at 09:30.

We got into the Temple of Olympian Zeus at 09:45 and there was no one else there, just us. We spent about 15 to 20 minutes on site, and that was plenty, as it's quite a small site. The main temple had scaffolding around it, and entry was €20 per person. To be honest, it was a bit disappointing. The views were probably just as good from outside the fence by the Arch of Hadrian, so it's worth considering how much more you'll get out of paying the entrance fee, or whether you'd be happy with the view from outside.

![Temple of Zeus](/images/trips/greece/athens/temple-zeus.webp)

We then walked around to the Panathenaic Stadium, which hosted the first modern Olympic Games. We got there at 10:10 and entry was €12 per person. I really enjoyed it, and the small museum was really interesting. It was great seeing the official posters from each Olympic Games over the years. We left the stadium at 10:50.

<figure>

![Panathenaic Stadium](/images/trips/greece/athens/panathenaic-stadium.webp)

<figcaption>Panathenaic Stadium</figcaption>

At 11:45 we caught the funicular up Lycabettus. The return ticket was €13 per person. We had a wander around and enjoyed the views, had a drink at the restaurant at the top and caught the funicular back down at 12:45.

<figure>

![View from Mt Lycabettus](/images/trips/greece/athens/lycabettus.webp)

We couldn't visit the National Garden because of demonstrations nearby. It was closed by police order.

At 13:30 we stopped for lunch at [Meat the Greek](https://meatthegreek.gr/) for the pork gyros, as we had read it was one of the best in Athens, and it didn't disappoint. We had two pork gyros and two beers for €16. It was delicious.

We had pre-booked tickets for the Acropolis at 15:00 as a birthday surprise. You book online before going and choose a time slot. I haven't looked at the price. Luckily our hotel was just a few minutes from the entrance, so we popped back and chilled out for half an hour.

<figure>

![Parthenon](/images/trips/greece/athens/parthenon.webp)

We got to the Acropolis at 15:00 and left at about 16:45. It wasn't overly busy at that time and it never felt crowded.

At 17:00 we went to the [Hyper Astro bar](https://hyperathens.gr/) for rooftop terrace views of the Acropolis. We had Aperol spritzes and tasty appetisers and enjoyed the views. After drinks on the terrace we went back to the hotel for a rest before heading out for dinner at 20:30.

We went to [Karamanlidika](https://www.karamanlidika.gr/en/), about a 20-minute walk from our hotel. It's a small deli and restaurant in the Psyrri area of Athens, known for its cured meats, which you can also buy at the deli counter. The food was really tasty and it was really good value. We were given a complimentary starter of cold cuts and cheese when we sat down, and at the end of the meal we were given complimentary thick Greek yoghurt topped with candied carrot, which was a lovely touch. It's at Sokratous 1 and Evripidou 52, and it's worth searching the name on Google Maps for current opening hours and reviews.

**Day 3 · Lake Vouliagmeni · Temple of Poseidon · Acropolis Museum · Departure**

We had a late flight back to the UK that evening, so we had breakfast at the hotel that morning. Athens is quite small, and after a day and a half of exploring we felt we had done everything we wanted to, so a bus tour was a good way to spend our last day. It was a birthday surprise, booked through GetYourGuide, so I haven't looked at the price. The tour only included the bus, and we had to pay entry for the lake and the Temple of Poseidon ourselves. It picked us up at the hotel entrance at 09:30. We checked out of our room but left our bags at the hotel.

The first stop was Lake Vouliagmeni, on the Athenian Riviera. We got there just after 10:00 and spent about an hour and a half. The lake is fed by thermal springs rising from deep underground, which mix with sea water, and it sits below a backdrop of cliffs. The water was supposed to be 23°C. It's said to range between about 22°C and 29°C through the year, so late October is probably towards the cooler end. It felt chillier to us, but it was bearable and only took a moment to adjust to. The water is mostly shallow and crystal clear.

The lake is home to Garra rufa fish, often called doctor fish, which nibble at dead skin if you sit around the edge. It's harmless and a bit ticklish, and if you don't like it you can move to deeper water, where there are fewer of them.

Basic entry was €17 per person. This entry meant you could use the seating area around the bar, but it was in shade, so we went and sat on the decking on the other side of the lake. You can buy upgrades for comfier seating areas. It's a fully commercial, well-run site rather than a wild swimming spot, with changing rooms, lockers and a café-bar and restaurant, but we didn't try out the bar or restaurant on our visit.

<figure>

![Lake Vouliagmeni](/images/trips/greece/athens/vouliagmeni.webp)

<figcaption>Lake Vouliagmeni</figcaption>
</figure>

When we arrived at about 10:00 it was very quiet. It was starting to get busier when we left, but still felt pretty empty with plenty of space. Our bus left at 11:45.

We drove further down the coast and got to the Temple of Poseidon at 12:30. We had an hour to wander around, which was just about enough time to cover the main area, although we wouldn't have minded a little more time to explore the wider area. Entry was €20 per person for adults. It was really peaceful when we were there, and it's in a beautiful spot, perched on top of a cliff at Cape Sounion, around 60 metres above the Aegean Sea. The temple is roped off, so you can't go inside it, but you can walk all the way around it. It was built around 440 BC, at about the same time as the Parthenon, and was dedicated to the god of the sea. Of the original 34 Doric columns, 15 are still standing. One of them has Lord Byron's name carved into it, left on a visit in 1810, and many other visitors have added their own signatures since. There is a café with great sea views, which we didn't use, and a small gift shop.

<figure>

![Temple of Poseidon](/images/trips/greece/athens/temple-poseidon.webp)

<figcaption>Temple of Poseidon</figcaption>
</figure>

We got back to Athens just after 15:00 and were dropped off near the National Garden. We hadn't seen it the day before, so we walked back through it.

We still had some time before we needed to head to the airport, so at 16:00 we went to the Acropolis Museum. Entry was €20 per person. We had a wander around the museum, then a drink on the rooftop terrace, which has views of the Acropolis. We left at about 17:00 and had dinner near our hotel.

We caught the metro back to the airport at 18:20. The journey took a little over an hour and cost about €10 per person.

## Practical Info

### Getting There & Away

**Arriving in Athens:** From the airport, follow signs from arrivals to the metro (Line 3, blue) heading for Syntagma, then change to Line 2 (red) for one stop to Acropoli. The journey takes about 40 minutes and costs around €9 per person — just tap in and tap out, no ticket needed. A taxi costs around €40–50.

**Leaving Athens:** The metro back to the airport took a little over an hour and cost about €10 per person.

---

### Getting Around

Central Athens is compact and very walkable — most sights, restaurants, and the hotel were all within easy walking distance of each other. The metro is cheap, simple, and tap-in/tap-out with no physical ticket needed. For sights further out (Lake Vouliagmeni, Temple of Poseidon), a half-day bus tour was an easy way to cover more ground on a short trip.

---

### Money

Euros (€). Cards are widely accepted, there didn't seem to be any need for cash. Entry fees for sights add up quickly — most major sites were €20 per adult.

---

### What to Book in Advance

**Acropolis tickets** — book online in advance and choose a time slot. Ours was booked for 15:00 and it never felt crowded at that time.

**Day tours** — if you want to cover sights further from the centre (like Lake Vouliagmeni and the Temple of Poseidon) in a single day, book a bus tour ahead of time. Ours was through GetYourGuide.

### Tips & Things to Know

**The Temple of Olympian Zeus may not be worth the entry fee.** It's a small site, and the view from outside the fence by the Arch of Hadrian looked just as good to us. Worth weighing up before you pay.

**The National Garden can close without notice.** Ours was shut by police order because of nearby demonstrations — worth having a backup plan if you're relying on it.

**Lake Vouliagmeni has doctor fish.** Garra rufa fish nibble at dead skin around the edges of the lake — harmless and a bit ticklish. Move to deeper water if it's not for you. Water temperature varies seasonally (around 22–29°C); late October felt on the cooler side.

**Book Acropolis tickets for a specific time slot.** Ours at 15:00 never felt crowded.

**Good food, no reservations drama.** To Kati Allo (Chatzichristou 12) and [Karamanlidika](https://www.karamanlidika.gr/en/) (Sokratous 1 / Evripidou 52) were both excellent and inexpensive. To Kati Allo has no website, but it's easy to find on Google Maps.

**Hyper Astro bar is a good spot for rooftop views of the Acropolis** — good for an Aperol spritz and appetisers in the early evening.
