## Overview

**Where:** Guatemala & Belize
**When:** May 2026
**Duration:** 16 days
**Getting around:** Shuttle buses, one internal flight, hire car, boats
**Type of trip:** Self-directed — no package, no group tour

---

Guatemala and Belize don't always get mentioned in the same breath, but as a two-week trip they fit together almost perfectly. Two countries, completely different in character — volcanic highlands and Mayan ruins giving way to jungle rivers, caves, and eventually the reef. Jungle, ruins, reef. Hard to beat.

The trip splits naturally into three parts. Guatemala takes up the first week: Antigua as a gentle landing pad, Lake Atitlán for a couple of days on the water, then north to Flores and a full day at Tikal. From there you cross into Belize, pick up a hire car in San Ignacio, and spend several days on the Hummingbird Highway — one of the better decisions of the trip. The final stretch is Caye Caulker: small, unhurried, and a good place to finish.

May is the start of the rainy season in both countries. In practice this meant hot, sunny days — touching 35–40°C — with no rain to speak of until a brief downpour arriving into Flores. Off-season also means quieter sites and noticeably lower accommodation prices, particularly on Caye Caulker.

A word on pace: this itinerary moves. There are several early starts, a few long travel days, and a lot packed into each day. That suited us — but if you want a more relaxed pace, you could easily drop Hopkins, spend an extra night on the lake, or add a day in San Ignacio.

**Who it suits:**
People who like doing things rather than sitting by a pool. You don't need to be particularly fit — nothing here is technically demanding — but you do need to be comfortable with an early alarm, a bit of heat, and the odd rough road.

## Day by Day

### Guatemala

---

**Day 1 · Arrival in Antigua**

Landed late and transferred straight to Antigua by private shuttle — around an hour from the airport, costing roughly £40. There's not much to say about a 9.30pm arrival other than: get to the hotel, get some sleep.

---

**Day 2 · Exploring Antigua**

Jet lag had us up early, which turned out to be no bad thing — Antigua is best in the morning before the heat builds. We were out by half eight and spent the next few hours working through the main sights. Most have a small entry fee of around £4 a head and none need more than 30–45 minutes. The market near the arch is worth a proper look if you're after souvenirs — better than most of the gift shops dotted around town.

Antigua is small. Genuinely small. Compact cobbled streets, pretty courtyards, with a constant stream of traffic. By early afternoon we'd seen what there was to see, so we headed back to the hotel, used the pool, had a nap, and treated the second half of the day as a rest day.

Late afternoon we walked up to the viewing platform — an easy 25-minute stroll — then found a rooftop bar for a cocktail before an early night. Before bed we booked the Pacaya volcano shuttle for the morning through the hotel reception. Around £15 per person, with a 6am departure to beat the worst of the heat.

---

**Day 3 · Pacaya Volcano**

The shuttle picked us up just after 6am, made a few more hotel pickups, then stopped briefly at a small coffee bar on the edge of town before an hour's drive to Pacaya. Worth grabbing something here if you haven't eaten — there's not much at the trailhead.

At the entrance to the national park there's an additional fee of 100 quetzales per person on top of the tour cost. Our group was about ten people, all from the same bus, with one guide.

The walk itself is through trees for most of the ascent — a well-worn path, soft and loose underfoot in places but entirely manageable in a decent pair of trainers. It took around an hour to reach the open ridge with views across the lava fields to Pacaya. From there the path continues through the lava fields to a lower crater, where the guides toast marshmallows over the residual heat. It's a good moment. We descended via a different route to a second car park and were back in Antigua just after noon.

Honest verdict: the lava fields are genuinely impressive and standing in the centre of a crater with the edges rising up around you is pretty cool. But go in with realistic expectations — you're not summiting Pacaya, you're not getting panoramic views from the top, and a lot of the reviews make it sound more dramatic than it is. Worth doing, just not the standout experience some write-ups suggest.

Back in Antigua we had lunch at Linda's, used the pool for the afternoon, and booked the next morning's shuttle to Panajachel from the hotel reception. 280 quetzales for two.

---

**Day 4 · Travel to Lake Atitlán · San Marcos · San Pedro**

The shuttle picked us up at 8.35am — bags on the roof, fourteen seats, all taken. A toilet stop at a garage after two hours, then into Panajachel by noon. Hotel Tzanjuyú was only a few minutes' walk from where the shuttle drops off at the boats, which made things easy.

We caught the 1pm boat to San Marcos — 30 quetzales per person, about 35 minutes with a couple of stops. San Marcos is touristy and compact: a long street of food places and souvenir shops leading back from the dock. We found the Hidden Garden for lunch, tucked away in a quiet courtyard, and had a really good burrito.

In the afternoon we tried to visit the Cerro Tzankujil reserve — the spot with the cliff jumping and diving platforms — but arrived to find it already closing up despite the listed 4.30pm closing time. With limited options we jumped a boat to San Pedro instead (15 minutes) but didn't find much there worth lingering for. Had a beer by the lake and headed back to Panajachel by 4.30.

---

**Day 5 · San Juan · Swimming at San Marcos**

Started the day with San Juan, which is the pick of the villages — more space, more colour, less tourist tat than San Marcos or San Pedro. From the dock it's a walk up a hill lined with shops and small cafes. We found a quiet spot called Maria's for breakfast: two breakfasts with coffee and a smoothie for 140 quetzales. Had a wander around town then headed back to the boats.

From San Juan we took a boat back to San Marcos for swimming at the Cerro Tzankujil reserve — 40 quetzales per person to get in. Spent a couple of hours there: trails, cliff jumping if you want it, and areas where you can simply climb into the lake. Good spot. Left San Marcos at 4.30 and were back in Panajachel by 5pm.

---

**Day 6 · Atitlán Nature Reserve · Travel to Flores**

A long one. Our flight from Guatemala City to Flores wasn't until 9pm, so the morning was free — we checked out and walked over to the Atitlán Nature Reserve, about 25 minutes from the hotel. Entry was 80 quetzales per person. The trails take around 45 minutes to an hour and we got lucky with a group of coatis. There's a zipwire at the end which we skipped. A good way to fill a morning before a travel day.

The shuttle bus timetable didn't work with our flight, so we booked a private taxi to Guatemala City — $100, picking us up just after 4pm. We arrived at the airport at 7.20pm; the flight left at 9.10pm and took under half an hour to reach Flores.

Outside the airport a shared shuttle to the island costs 25 quetzales per person. We hadn't realised it was a shared vehicle until we got in, but it filled quickly and dropped us just the other side of the bridge — a short walk to the hotel from there.

---

**Day 7 · Tikal**

The shuttle picked us up at the bridge at 8.10am. We'd bought our Tikal tickets online before leaving the UK — recommended, as the queue at the ticket office on arrival was long. There are snack shops at the park entrance but the food isn't worth your time; bring your own or eat properly before you leave.

From the entrance it's another 30-minute drive to the site itself. Our tour group ended up being ten people, and the guide was excellent — knowledgeable about Mayan history, archaeology, and the site's ecology, and good at keeping things moving. The walking part of the tour takes just over four hours and covers around six miles. It is hot, the temple climbs are steep, and the walk back to the entrance from the final site is 45 minutes. Bring plenty of water. The wildlife — toucans, coatis, howler and spider monkeys — is a genuine highlight rather than a side note.

One thing to be aware of: the itinerary implies you'll be back in Flores by 3pm. You won't. After the tour there's a compulsory stop at the park entrance restaurant, which is a tourist trap with limited options and a tendency to drag on for close to an hour while those who pre-ordered food get seated. We grabbed sandwiches from a hatch and waited. We didn't leave until 4pm and were back in Flores just before 5.

Flores itself is tiny — 20 minutes to walk the whole island. There's not much to do but it's pretty enough, with some colourful buildings and decent lake views. We'd nearly planned a full 24 hours here; glad we didn't. An hour or so to explore, a shower, sunset, and dinner was exactly the right amount.

---

### Belize

---

**Day 8 · Border Crossing · San Ignacio**

An early start. The shuttle from Flores left at 6.10am — worth knowing that this service continues on to Mexico, which is why the departure time is non-negotiable. It cost £15 per person and was noticeably more comfortable than the Guatemalan shuttles: a proper coach in a 2-1 seating formation rather than a crammed minibus.

They hand out immigration forms on the bus. They do not hand out pens. Bring one.

We reached the border just before 8am. A man boarded the bus to give instructions and handle currency exchange — not sure if this was the best rate available but no alternatives were offered. From there it was a short drive to the crossing itself, where you collect your bags and walk through both Guatemalan and Belizean passport control. The whole process took around 25 minutes and was straightforward. The bus was waiting on the Belize side and left once everyone had crossed, departing around 9.10am. We were in San Ignacio by 9.30.

We left our bags at the shuttle office — the man there said he'd be around until 8pm — and headed out on foot. First stop was the Green Iguana Conservation Project, a short walk from the centre. Tours run on the hour, last around 45 minutes, and you get to handle iguanas that can't be released into the wild. The 11am tour was genuinely good. We had a drink at the bar there afterwards then walked up the hill to Cahal Pech — another 20 minutes or so. Entry was 20 Belize dollars per person. Small museum, a handful of ruins, and we had the entire site to ourselves. Spotted toucans.

Back down to town took about 25 minutes via a more direct route. Grabbed lunch, collected our bags from the shuttle office, and caught a cab to the hotel, checking in around 3.30pm.

---

**Day 9 · Blue Hole · St Herman's Cave · Sleeping Giant**

Today was the day we picked up the hire car, which immediately changed the feel of the trip — suddenly everything was on our own schedule. First stop was the Lamanai Chocolate shop, but they don't run tours on Sundays so we just had a drink and moved on.

We checked into the Sleeping Giant resort to drop bags, then drove back to St Herman's Blue Hole National Park. The blue hole itself is a short walk from the car park along a stepped path — a natural pool of startlingly clear, cool water, a genuine relief in the heat. There were only a handful of other people when we arrived. We stayed in the water for about half an hour.

A few miles further up the road is the car park for St Herman's Cave. Entry is 8 Belize dollars per person. There are two trail options — moderate (15 minutes) or easy (a few minutes shorter) — and we took the moderate, which had some roots and rocks underfoot but nothing that required anything more than comfortable trainers. The cave entrance involves a steep staircase down. You can explore the first 100 metres or so independently; beyond that you need to be part of a tubing group.

A note on torches: the ranger checks you have one before letting you through. We'd left our head torches in our bags at the hotel and got waved through on phone torches, but it wasn't ideal. The cave is enormous and the phone light doesn't do justice to the scale of it. If you can remember one thing from these notes, make it this: bring a proper head torch.

We were back at the Sleeping Giant by 3pm. The resort is a step up in price from where we'd been staying, but it earns it — pool open 24 hours, river access with kayaks, trails through the grounds, rope bridges. We had an early dinner at the resort around 4.30pm (pizza, pricey, excellent) then walked up to the hilltop gazebo to watch the light fade over the valley. Good evening.

---

**Day 10 · ATM Cave · Night Hike**

The ATM Cave — Actun Tunichil Muknal — is the highlight of the Belize section and one of the best things we did on the entire trip. Book before you leave home. Numbers are strictly limited and it sells out.

We drove ourselves to Teakettle village to meet the tour, as arranged in advance. The bus stops there anyway for people to pick up water and snacks — the shop has crisps and not much else, so eat a proper breakfast before you leave. From Teakettle it's around 40 minutes to the base camp, where there are changing facilities and groups are split up. Each guide takes six visitors.

It's then a 45-minute walk to the cave entrance along an easy path with three river crossings, the deepest of which comes up to about waist height. At the entrance there's a short swim to get inside — and from there the tour speaks for itself. We won't go into detail because the less you know going in, the better. What we'll say is this: our guide was exceptional, the archaeology is extraordinary, and three hours inside didn't feel like enough. It never felt crowded despite multiple groups being in there at once.

You can't take your own camera inside, but you fill out a form at the start with your email address and they send photos afterwards.

We were back at base camp around 2pm. Lunch of rice and chicken is included, which was welcome after the morning's exertion. Back at the hotel just after 4pm, swim in the pool, and then a night hike booked through the resort — departing at 7.30pm. The guide drove us a short distance then led a 45-minute walk through the jungle. We saw scorpions, plenty of other insects, and various nocturnal activity. No jaguars, but that was never a realistic expectation. Back at the hotel by 9.15pm.

---

**Day 11 · Caracol · Rio On Pools · Big Rock Falls**

A big day, and a good one. Everything written online about the road to Caracol describes a rough dirt track — that information is now out of date. The road is fully paved and in excellent condition. We left the Sleeping Giant at 7.30am, reached the Mountain Pine Ridge checkpoint at 8.45am (rangers take your name and car details), and were at Caracol by 9.30am. The drive took two hours from our starting point on the Hummingbird Highway — further than San Ignacio, so allow slightly less from there.

Caracol is bigger than Tikal in terms of the original site, but far less has been excavated. There's a tidy reception building, clean toilets, and a small museum at the entrance. No café, no shop, no tour guides available on site unless you've pre-arranged one. We had the place almost entirely to ourselves and spent around an hour and twenty minutes exploring before heading back to the car just after 11am.

Rio On Pools was next — about 30 minutes' drive back down the road. A short walk from the car park leads to a series of natural pools and small waterfalls carved into the granite. There are changing facilities on site. We spent an hour and a half here, working our way between pools, and had the place largely to ourselves. Left at 1.30pm.

Ten minutes further down the road is the turn-off for Big Rock Falls. From there it's another ten minutes along a rougher track — fine in dry conditions, but you'd want a 4x4 if it had rained recently. From the car park it's a steep five-minute descent to the bottom, where two large pools sit beneath a substantial waterfall. Deep enough to jump into. We spent about an hour there.

Rio On Pools and Big Rock Falls are completely different experiences and worth doing both if you can. Rio On is shallow and calm — good for lounging. Big Rock is deeper, colder, and better for swimming and jumping. We were back through the Mountain Pine Ridge checkpoint by 3.30pm, stopped briefly at Martha's for food just beyond the gate, then drove the final stretch to San Ignacio arriving around 4.30pm.

We dropped the hire car just after 5pm and walked into town for a beer and dinner at the Guava Limbo café — we'd eaten there earlier in the trip and it was just as good second time around. A few notes on the hire car decision: a lot of blogs recommend basing yourself in San Ignacio and doing everything from there. We'd push back on that. Having the car and staying on the Hummingbird Highway gave us somewhere genuinely nice to return to each afternoon, with the pool, the river, and the trails keeping us entertained in the evenings. San Ignacio is fine for a night or two but we wouldn't want five days there.

**Day 13 · Travel to Caye Caulker**

Up early to catch the bus to Belize City and then the boat to Caye Caulker. We'd booked through GetYourGuide, which covered both the bus and the boat as one ticket.

The pickup was listed as 9.30am with a request to be in the hotel lobby from 9.15. We were collected at 9.40am, but the driver took us to the main office first to sign in before the actual departure — our hotel was only a few minutes' walk from the office, so this was slightly unnecessary, but it wasn't a big deal. We left San Ignacio at 9.45am and arrived at the ferry terminal in Belize City at 12.20pm.

The GetYourGuide ticket caused a small amount of confusion at the terminal. The driver escorted everyone to the ticket desk and then left — we appeared to be the only ones who'd already paid, and nobody offered guidance on what to do next. It took a bit of hunting to figure out that we needed to go to a small shop called Atlanta in the terminal to collect the physical tickets, then take those to the main office to get them stamped. Worth knowing in advance. Once sorted, luggage was checked in and we had about 20 minutes before the 1.30pm boat, which was enough time to grab something from one of the cafés inside the terminal.

We arrived at Caye Caulker at 2.30pm. Golf cart taxis outside the terminal quoted 20 Belize dollars to the hotel — we walked instead, about 15 minutes. Checked in, went for a wander around the island, and had an early night.

---

**Day 14 · Snorkelling the Barrier Reef**

The best day of the trip. We'd booked a private snorkelling tour through the hotel — $195 per person — and it was worth every cent.

The guide picked us up from the pier right outside the hotel at 10am and dropped us back at 3pm. In that time we covered a serious amount of reef: sting rays, loggerhead turtles, green turtles, nurse sharks, and more fish than we could keep track of. There was a manatee in the area too — we anchored at a safe distance and swam slowly over to where it had been spotted. A boat from one of the larger operators came in far too close and spooked it before we got there. Frustrating, and avoidable.

For most of the spots the water was shallow, warm, and completely clear. The guide was in the water with us at some points, pointing things out; at others he stayed on the boat and let us explore. Lunch and drinks were included. It never felt rushed.

A note on confidence in the water: this tour works even if you're not a strong swimmer. The conditions were calm and the guide was attentive. None of the spots were particularly deep.

---

**Day 15 · North Island · The Split**

A quieter day. After breakfast we caught the water taxi across to the north island — 5 Belize dollars per person return, running back and forth every few minutes. The boat driver pointed us toward Kiki King beach: follow the signs for Bondi Beach Club, don't go into the resort, and there's a small free beach immediately alongside it with seating in the shade. It's a few minutes' walk from where the taxi drops you. The north side is more sheltered from the breeze, which in May means very hot — we headed back to the south island around noon.

The afternoon was spent around the Split — swimming in the sea, lunch, and a wander over to Iguana Reef to look at the seahorse habitat. We'd read that stingray feeding happens here around 4.30pm; when we arrived there were already several rays in the water so we had a look at those instead. The seahorses are apparently tiny and easy to miss — we couldn't find any and gave up fairly quickly in the heat, which is about the right attitude to take.

By 3pm we retreated to the hotel pool, which had the considerable advantage of being shaded.

---

**Day 16 · Departure**

We'd booked the 8.30am ferry but it didn't leave until 9.45am. The crossing to Belize City cost 36 Belize dollars per person.

We'd also pre-booked a shuttle from the ferry terminal to the airport — 15 USD per person via WhatsApp — but had booked it from the wrong terminal. The right terminal was only a four-minute walk away, but the boat had already docked at the other one and the shuttle had come and gone. A quick message on WhatsApp confirmed there wasn't another one in time, so we grabbed a taxi instead at $25 for both of us. Arrived at the airport by 11am, through security with time to spare. Flight home at 1.30pm.

## Practical Info

### Getting There & Away

**Into Guatemala:** Flying into Guatemala City (GUA) is the standard entry point. A private transfer to Antigua costs around £40 and takes about an hour — bookable through your hotel or at the airport. Shuttle buses are cheaper but slower and less straightforward late at night.

**Internal flight:** We flew from Guatemala City to Flores with TAG Airlines. The flight takes under 30 minutes and costs around £50–60 per person booked in advance. There are shuttle buses from Antigua to Flores but they take the best part of a day — the flight is worth it.

**Into Belize:** The shuttle from Flores to San Ignacio costs around £15 per person and takes approximately three and a half hours including the border crossing. Comfortable, straightforward, and the easiest option. Bring a pen for the immigration form.

**Home from Belize:** We flew home from Philip Goldson International Airport in Belize City. The water taxi from Caye Caulker to the ferry terminal in Belize City is 36 Belize dollars per person. Allow at least two hours between the boat docking and your flight departing — the ferry runs late and the taxi from the terminal to the airport costs around $25 USD.

---

### Getting Around

**Guatemala:** Shuttle buses between tourist destinations are efficient and cheap — book through your hotel reception the evening before. They fill up fast and popular routes run once a day. For Antigua to Panajachel, expect to pay around 140 quetzales for two people. Lake Atitlán villages are connected by regular boats — 25–50 quetzales per journey depending on destination.

**Belize:** Hire a car. Seriously. The Hummingbird Highway and everything along it — Blue Hole, St Herman's Cave, Caracol, Rio On Pools, Big Rock Falls — is either very difficult or completely impossible without one. San Ignacio has several car hire options. A standard 4x4 is worth the extra cost given some of the tracks off the main road. Drop-off was straightforward with no issues.

**Caye Caulker:** Golf cart taxis are everywhere and quote around 20 Belize dollars for most journeys on the island. In practice, almost everything is walkable — the island is tiny. The water taxi to the north island runs constantly and costs 5 Belize dollars return.

---

### Money

Three currencies across one trip, which sounds more complicated than it is in practice.

**Guatemala:** Guatemalan quetzales (GTQ). Cards are accepted in most restaurants and hotels in tourist areas, but carry cash for boats, entrance fees, shuttle buses, and tips. ATMs are available in Antigua and Panajachel; harder to find in Flores. At time of writing, roughly 10 quetzales to £1.

**Belize:** Belize dollars (BZD), fixed at 2:1 to the US dollar. USD is accepted almost everywhere and often preferred. Many prices — particularly tours and accommodation — are quoted in USD. Cards work in most hotels and larger restaurants; carry cash for smaller purchases, entrance fees, and transport.

**Border crossing:** There's a currency exchange on the bus at the Guatemala-Belize border. We used it for convenience — not sure it offers the best rate, but it was the only option presented to us. Worth having a small amount of USD already on you before the crossing.

---

### What to Book in Advance

A few things genuinely need to be sorted before you leave home:

**ATM Cave** — strictly limited daily numbers and it sells out weeks ahead, particularly in peak season. Book as early as possible. Don't leave this one to chance.

**Tikal tickets** — available online and strongly recommended. The queue at the ticket office on arrival is long and it eats into your morning. Buy online, save the hassle.

**Snorkelling tour, Caye Caulker** — private tours from the smaller operators book up quickly, especially in peak season. We booked through our hotel. If you want a private rather than group tour, sort this before you arrive on the island.

**Internal flight, Guatemala City to Flores** — book ahead for the best prices and to guarantee your preferred timing.

### Tips & Things to Know

**Bring a head torch.** Specifically for St Herman's Cave, but useful throughout. The ranger will check you have one. Phone torches technically pass but they're not adequate for the scale of the cave.

**Tikal takes longer than advertised.** Factor in the compulsory post-tour restaurant stop at the park entrance — it added nearly an hour to our day. If you have onward transport or a flight, build in buffer.

**The road to Caracol is now fully paved.** Everything online says otherwise — ignore it. We drove a standard hire car without any issues. The track to Big Rock Falls is a different matter and benefits from a 4x4 if there's been recent rain.

**Hopkins isn't worth a detour.** Pleasant enough for a beer and a sea breeze, but there's nothing to do there. Unless you're specifically planning to stay overnight or have something organised, skip it and spend the time at Rio On Pools instead.

**May is off-season.** Quieter sites, lower accommodation prices, and hot sunny weather. It rained once — arriving into Flores. If you're considering May, don't let the rainy season label put you off.

**San Juan is the best of the Atitlán villages.** More colourful, more space, less tourist-oriented than San Marcos or San Pedro. Start there.

**Book Caye Caulker transport carefully.** There are multiple ferry terminals in Belize City and they're not all the same. Make sure any pre-booked shuttle or taxi knows exactly which terminal your boat arrives at.
